PIP FOR KIDS

A voice box of their very own.

Pip lives on a palm-sized box with a bright little screen. Press, talk, and your Pip whooshes off to Mom or Grandma with a chime.

One button to talk, one to listen. That is the whole manual.
Names, not menus. Kids tap the person, not an interface.
No camera, no browser, no store. It only knows your family and it only does one thing: Pip.
Bring your own box
Pip runs on an off-the-shelf mini board (we like the Waveshare ESP32-S3 Touch 1.8″, about €35). Order it online. When it arrives, plug it into your computer, open __HOST__, and press one button. Two minutes later the bare little gadget is a member of the family. No soldering, no downloads, no shipping it anywhere - if you can plug in a USB cable, you're overqualified.

How it works

Three steps, and one of them is just shopping.

1

Grown-ups install the app

Open __HOST__ on your phone, add it to your home screen, sign in with a 6-digit email code.

2

Kids get their box

Order the board, and when it lands on your doormat, plug it into your computer and click “Make it a Pip.” Your family gets stamped in right there. Then plug it in anywhere with WiFi.

3

Pip

Press, speak, release. A chime rings on the other side. Repeat forever.

From doormat to first message: about five minutes.

No setup wizards. No manuals. Nobody's warehouse. Pinky promise.

PIP KEEPS SECRETS

Heard, then gone.

Pip is a messenger, not a recorder. Voice notes exist to be played, not kept.

Messages that melt. The moment a Pip is heard, we delete it from our servers. Unheard Pips evaporate after 30 days (but can always be re-sent). There is no archive, because there is no archive.
We know almost nothing about you. One email address per grown-up. No kid accounts, no names, no birthdays, no profiles. You can't leak what you never collected.
No ads. No trackers. No funny business. Your family is the customer, never the product. It's free, and we'll try hard to keep it that way.
Uncompromising on privacy. Every Pip travels encrypted and rests on servers under European privacy law, until it's played and gone.

The fine print, still in plain words: our privacy policy.
