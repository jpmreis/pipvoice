Pip

Privacy, in plain words.

Last updated 21 August 2026

Pip is a tiny voice-message service for families, run by a family. The honest summary: your voice messages pass through our server on their way to your people, and then we delete them. We keep almost nothing, because we want almost nothing.

This Pip is self-hosted: it runs on your own family's server, set up by someone you know, not by a company. The honest summary: your voice messages pass through that server on their way to your people, and then they are deleted. It keeps almost nothing, because it wants almost nothing.

Voice messages don't stay on our server

A message lives on our server only while it is in transit:

  • Once it has been delivered and heard, it is deleted from our server within about a day. That short wait is just so it can still reach your other phone if you use Pip on more than one.
  • If it is never picked up - a box unplugged for the summer, say - it is deleted after 30 days, unheard.
  • Delete a message in the app and it is removed from our server right away.

A kid's Pip box keeps its own copy of the messages it has received, on the box itself, in your home - that inbox is yours, not ours. Messages older than 30 days fade off the box too.

What we keep about you

The little that makes Pip work:

  • Your email address (to sign you in with a 6-digit code) and the name and colour your family sees.
  • Who in your family may talk to whom - the guest list a grown-up sets.
  • Reactions (the heart, the HAHA), and whether a box has recently been online.
  • If you turn notifications on: the delivery address your phone's push service gives us. The notification itself only says someone sent you a message - never the audio.
  • One cookie, which keeps you signed in. That is the whole cookie story.

Sign-in codes and device keys are stored hashed, so even we can't read them back.

What we never do

  • No ads, no analytics, no trackers.
  • No selling or sharing your data, and no training AI on your voices.
  • No listening: nobody at Pip plays your messages.
  • No strangers: only the people on your family's guest list can ever reach you or your kids.

Backups

We keep short-lived backups so a server accident can't eat the family's messages in transit. Backups are kept for up to two weeks, which means a deleted message can linger inside a backup for up to two weeks after it disappears from the server. Then it is really, truly gone.

The few services we lean on

Hetzner

Our server lives in a Hetzner data centre in Germany, in the EU. Everything travels to and from it encrypted.

Resend

Sends our emails: your sign-in code, and the occasional "you have an unheard message" nudge.

The few services this Pip leans on

Your family's server

Everything runs on a server your own family operates. Where it lives, and which email service (if any) sends sign-in codes and nudges, is your family's choice.

Apple, Google & Mozilla push

Your phone's own notification service delivers the "new Pip!" poke. It never carries the audio.

ip-api.com

When a Pip box starts up it asks this service for its timezone, so the clock on the box is right. The service sees the box's IP address and nothing else.

Kids

Pip is built for kids, so the box is deliberately boring: no camera, no browser, no store, no feed. It only knows the family members a grown-up has chosen, and it only does one thing: Pip.
